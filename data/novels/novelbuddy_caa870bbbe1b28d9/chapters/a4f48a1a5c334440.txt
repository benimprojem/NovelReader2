Chapter 17: The World Begins to Break

A week slipped by after the summit broke apart. Not a single order issued by the leaders escaped Silas’s planetary senses, and the world outside was fracturing at the seams. His awareness sank deeper into the earth with every rotation; he held de facto control of the globe, though his current reach was a fraction of what would unlock once the World Spirit opened its eyes.

Authoritarian states locked down their borders and disarmed their citizens with mechanical speed. Their people were already accustomed to sweeping state mandates, leaving little room for organized pushback.

The democracies, however, blew apart into chaos.

Protest, marches, clogged capital cities, and riots flared across public squares. People knew their elected officials worked under constitutional limits, and the sudden, warrantless stripping of their personal liberties brought fury into the streets.

Under emergency directives, police forces and army battalions worked day and night, stripping firearms, ammunition crates, and industrial explosives from depots and private homes. Licensed or black-market, it made no difference; everything was loaded into trucks. Oddly, people caught hiding unregistered weapons were not thrown into prison cells. Their guns were simply logged on clipboards and carried down into concrete bunkers.

News networks ran continuous emergency coverage, experts screaming over why every government on the planet was simultaneously gutting its own military. Meanwhile, the scientific community was dealing with its own confusion. Atmospheric monitors and ocean probes were recording impossible data: the ozone layer was stitching itself together, industrial rivers were clearing up within days, and global vegetation was surging with unnatural vigor.

Among the ultra-wealthy, panic turned quiet and paranoid. Corporate summits were canceled, private jets were grounded, and old-money dynasties pulled their families into fortified country compounds.

Even the citizens under military regimes grew nervous. If the state was disarming the police and regular army, it should have been the perfect moment to start a rebellion, yet the confiscated rifles were clearly being stacked in storage rather than melted down. Everyone knew a massive shift was coming, even if they could not see the hand turning the wheel.

Online forums drowned in conspiracies, the most popular theory claiming that an alien battle fleet had parked in orbit and demanded Earth’s arsenal as tribute.

Through all of this, Silas stayed at home in absolute quiet.

Every evening, a fresh five-star meal arrived at their doorstep, sent directly by the Prime Minister’s personal staff.

Vijay had returned to his administrative seat in New Delhi, but after watching Silas move, he had abandoned any thought of fighting back. Instead, he tried to ingratiate himself however he could, even offering to move Silas’s family into a heavily guarded government estate.

Silas had turned him down immediately.

He cared deeply for Clara and Alice, but sticking them in a luxury bunker would only turn them into helpless targets down the line. Real survival in the cosmos came from personal strength and iron comprehension. If he sheltered them from the path, he was essentially leaving them defenseless against whatever came next. The wider universe held incredible wonders, but you had to be strong enough to survive the crossing to see them.

Moving along the Mentalist Path took far more than passively drinking spiritual energy; it required a cold, clear understanding of one’s own nature. Silas knew that better than anyone. His own Will was rock solid, yet it had taken a million years of library slavery to figure out who his real oppressor was.

For now, Silas watched the board. As society buckled under the new laws, real talent would start floating to the surface: people whose Wills had been choked out by the mundane world, finally finding room to breathe.

Inside the house, Clara and Alice were growing more stressed by the day.

First came the surreal sight of world leaders sitting in their dusty backyard. Now, the news was broadcasting planetary riots while five-star banquets appeared on their dining table every single night.

They knew Silas was at the middle of it, but whenever they tried to ask, he gave them vague answers that made them want to pull their hair out. Even so, they could not bring themselves to yell at him; they were terrified that dangerous people were using him.

Clara had settled on a theory that kept her sane: Silas had invented some revolutionary scientific technology, and the government had forced him to hand it over.

That evening, the three of them sat around the low dining table. The food smelled incredible, but Clara left her plate untouched. Alice looked at her mother, hesitated, and reluctantly set her fork down, staring at the steaming curry with her arms crossed.

Clara looked straight at Silas; her eyes lined with exhaustion and maternal worry.

"Silas, you cannot keep shutting us out."

Silas held her gaze, saying nothing.

"If those men took something you built, you have to tell me," Clara said gently. "I know I can’t fight politicians, but we are a family. We deal with this together."

A quiet warmth settled in Silas’s chest. He loved them, but telling the truth was impossible. How was he supposed to tell a working-class mother that her adopted ten-year-old was a million-year-old soul using the planet as a weapon?

He had spent the last three days putting together an alternate story.

Looking directly into Clara’s eyes, Silas said, "Mom, I spoke with the World Spirit when I was home alone."

Clara blinked. "A World Spirit?"

"Yes," Silas said, completely straight-faced. "The conscious soul of Earth."

Clara stared at him, her forehead creasing. "So, I am supposed to believe that the soul of the entire planet skipped past eight billion adults just to have a chat with my ten-year-old boy?"

"That is correct, Mother," Silas said without flinching. "It taught me ancient arts and left me with specific tasks to complete."

Clara’s eyes narrowed. "Really? And did this planet soul mention that you were going to be an emperor?"

She leaned forward over the table. "Or did it mention that you are the worst liar I have ever met?"

Silas paused.

"You, see?" Silas cleared his throat, looking slightly to the side. "This is why I didn’t want to bring it up."

Clara let out a long, ragged sigh, her face softening into tired affection. "If you aren’t ready to tell me the real truth, Silas, I won’t pry."

"You will understand eventually, Mother," Silas murmured, picking up his spoon and starting on his dinner.

Over the coming year, Silas planned to stay out of the way. A comfortable world bred stagnation, but a world pushed to the edge of an abyss would force everyone to show their real colors. In the fires of the coming slaughter, the real pillars of his new order would stand up on their own.

In the meantime, Silas pulled more ambient energy into his skull, building up his reservoir of golden Will Fog to get ready for the second stage of physical refinement: the blood.
