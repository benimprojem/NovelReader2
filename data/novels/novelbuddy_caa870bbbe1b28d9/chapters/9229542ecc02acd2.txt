Chapter 18: The Secret Beneath Earth

Prime Minister Vijay was losing his grip on the country. The domestic backlash had blown past his worst-case scenarios.

He had assumed his parliamentary majority and political capital would let him push the disarmament bills through without breaking the state.

He had miscalculated completely.

The opposition parties jumped on the weapon seizures with vicious precision, running round-the-clock media campaigns accusing him of tearing up the constitution to crown himself a king.

With street protests growing louder outside the capital, a dangerous thought crossed Vijay’s mind: If they are already calling me a monarch, why shouldn’t I take the title for real?

The thought died as quickly as it came. Vijay had seen the look in Silas’s eyes when mortal politics came up, a flat, cold disdain. Silas would never let a mundane politician sit on the throne of a country other than the promised seven sovereign.

Knowing his political standing was collapsing, Vijay decided to tie his strength to an ancient relic.

Flanked by personal guards, Vijay rode an elevator down into the country’s most secure subterranean vault. When Silas first described Life Will Weapons, Vijay had felt a surge of raw greed. He had wanted the planet itself for a brief second before realizing that trying to wrestle Earth away from Silas was a quick way to get his head crushed.

Even if Silas gave up the control of Earth Vijay knew his connection was not with Earth. Vijay’s internal identity was tangled up in lineage, mythology, and religious history. He felt no real connection to raw soil and tectonic plates; his mind lived in the ancient epics, the gods, and the great warrior kings of his ancestry. What better weapon could he choose than an artifact from those legends?

Vijay stepped up to a display case in the center of the vault.

Sitting on a velvet cloth was a recurve composite bow, an ancient piece excavated by premier archaeological teams and linked to Arjuna, the legendary archer of the Mahabharata.

Vijay did not waste time. He pulled a thick strand of grey Will Fog from his forehead and drove it straight into the aged frame. The second his Will sank into the metal, a sharp, ringing hum went through his chest, locking an energetic tether between his soul and the weapon.

A grin spread across Vijay’s face.

Silas had claimed it would take a year to master the earth, but Vijay suspected the boy was exaggerating to keep them timid. Looking at his own progress, Vijay calculated that his bow would hit full Rank One synchronization within a week.

The rules were simple: the stronger the cultural and personal bond between a cultivator and their relic, the higher its starting potential. Even better, a Life Will Weapon grew alongside its master; whenever Vijay climbed a rank, the bow would evolve right along with him, it will just need small extra efforts.

All he needed now was to hire the best archery masters in the country and learn how to shoot.

What Vijay did not know was that Silas was watching the entire scene play out from hundreds of miles away. 𝕗𝐫𝚎𝗲𝘄𝐞𝕓𝐧𝕠𝘃𝕖𝐥.𝐜𝚘𝚖

Silas’s senses covered every inch of the Earth. When he had first skimmed Earth’s mythologies, he had suspected the old legends were not entirely fictional. The metallic composition of Arjuna’s bow proved it: the alloy was standard star-forged metal from the Lesser Zone.

Ancient cultivators from the Lesser Zone had visited Earth thousands of years ago. But they had died before they could trigger the world’s awakening. Waking a Barren World was a delicate process; you could not simply hand foreign systems to the natives, because foreign paths had zero natural resonance with the planetary core. A countdown toward planetary evolution only began when an indigenous inhabitant forged or awakened an original path from within the world itself.

Silas pulled his awareness back to monitor the broader board. Watching mortal politicians scramble was mildly entertaining, but their timing was terrible. He could read Vijay’s moves before the man even made them; the man’s Greedy Will made his psychology an open book.

Vijay’s condition made his cultivation move faster the more he took from his enemies. But because he had zero philosophical understanding of what greed actually meant beneath the surface, his skin refinement was crawling at an agonizing pace. Without a sudden flash of insight, it would take him a full year just to finish the first phase of his own skin refinement.

Given enough time, Vijay might have secured a grip on the subcontinent. But when Silas watched a private supersonic transport streak across Asian airspace toward the Indian border, carrying Qi Feng, he knew the Indian Prime Minister’s days were finished.
